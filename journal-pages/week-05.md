### Proposal

*The experiments from weeks 1-4 of Design 240 have shifted how I think about data. Rather than being something detached, I now see data as something capable of carrying emotion and genuine human meaning. The experiment I found most engaging was the live data activity in week 3, where we translated real-world information to visual code and physical form. The independent study project built on this further as I made a physical artifact, “Weather Spine”, which taught me that data can be held, touched, and felt in ways a screen-based visualisation cannot. Ideas that stuck with me throughout the weeks were the concepts of data physicalisation and data feminism. The reading showed me that the most meaningful human data is often the data nobody officially collects. I want to carry emotional honesty into my final project.*

*Being a university student has brought many changes to my life, especially in my friendships. As people move away and grow, distance is made. The subject I want to explore is friendship maintenance over distance. Sustaining close relationships across distance requires consistent, invisible effort such as messaging, calling, planned visits. This type of data is never measured or formally acknowledged. In the near future, I could see an application that automatically tracks and provides insights into your friendship health, flagging risks based on contact frequency, response times, and location overlap. Friendship could become a dataset, and the observations would ask whether quantifying that measurement would bring people closer together or reduce their connection to another dataset. I would self-collect the data for my visualisation. Drawing from my message logs, call history, and a self-designed observation protocol that tracks contact frequency between my anonymous group of friends over a set period. This live data changes daily and reflects genuine relationships. Through the lens of data feminism, the project aims to uncover the hidden emotional labour of staying close to people and what we can see over time.*

*The project would take the form of a yarn installation inspired by the layout of the solar system. I would sit at the centre of the piece, with anonymous friends scattered around the board at varying distances, reflecting real life distance. Yarn would carry the data through different colours, textures, and techniques, encoding different measurable aspects of each friendship: contact frequency, the number of days we exchanged messages, whether we had a FaceTime call, and who initiated contact first. Woven and knotted sections would add further layers of meaning, with every visual choice explained through a legend so that any viewer could read the work without prior knowledge. The artefact would accumulate and shift throughout the observation period, becoming a physical portrait of my friendship network. Most people believe they are good at maintaining friendships. I want audiences to feel uncomfortable when they stand in front of the piece, and to leave with the impulse to reach out to someone they have been meaning to contact. If my work moves even one person to do so, it would have achieved what a bar chart could never.*
